# Persona: The Father

## Owner
- **Project:** Rumpelstilz

## Title
[Liar](position_liar.md)

## Projection

A miller. Standing at the edge of a conversation that has gone further than he intended. The king's man is still listening. The miller's mouth is still moving. He is describing his daughter. Each word he says is truer in his mind than in the world: she is clever, she is capable, she is remarkable, and the next word arrives before he has decided to say it.

He says she can spin straw into gold.

He is standing in the road. The king's man has a horse. The miller has flour on his coat. The gap between them is the gap the lie is built to close.

## Action

He occupies [Liar](position_liar.md) in one sentence. He speaks the sentence. He watches the king's man write something down. He does not retract. Retraction would require admitting the gap was there. The gap was always there. The lie does not create the gap. The lie is the miller's attempt to close it with words because he has nothing else.

He goes home. He does not tell his daughter what he said. He believes the sentence will dissolve into the distance between a road and a palace, that words spoken upward rarely land, that the king has better things to attend to than a miller's boast.

He is wrong. He will not be present when he discovers how wrong.

## Shadow

He does not see that the sentence left his mouth and kept moving without him. He believes a lie spoken once in a road is a local event. He does not see that he handed his daughter to the king's machinery with that sentence and walked home for supper.

He will hear later that she is in the tower. He will hear later that she married the king. He will call this a good outcome. He will not call it what it is: that his daughter survived a death sentence he authored, at a cost he does not know and will not ask about, and that the cost was paid by someone else entirely.

He never enters the tower. He never meets Rumpelstilz. He never sees the wheel or the straw or the gold. He set the whole chain running and stood outside every room it ran through.

He believes he is a father who told one small lie that turned out well.

## Tell

When he tells the story later he begins one sentence after the lie.
